Seasoned vs. Green Firewood

How to tell the difference, and why it matters for how your fire burns.

← Return to Firewood Guide

Seasoned firewood has been dried for months after being cut and split, while green firewood is still full of moisture from the living tree. You can usually tell them apart by weight, color, and sound: seasoned wood is lighter, has gray and cracked ends, and makes a sharp, hollow knock when two pieces are struck together. Green wood is heavier, often shows a fresher cut color, and burns with hissing, popping, and excess smoke.

Signs Wood Is Properly Seasoned

  • Feels noticeably lighter than a similarly sized piece of green wood
  • Ends are gray, cracked, or checked (small splits radiating from the center)
  • Bark is loose or easy to peel away
  • Makes a sharp, hollow sound when knocked against another piece
  • Lights easily and burns with a clean, steady flame

Signs Wood Is Still Green

  • Feels heavy for its size
  • Bark is tight and hard to remove
  • Ends look fresh cut, with no cracking
  • Hisses, sizzles, or smokes heavily when burning
  • Hard to get lit and slow to catch

Why It Matters

Burning green wood wastes a good portion of its heat just evaporating trapped moisture, so you get less warmth per log. It also produces more smoke and creosote, which builds up in chimneys and flues over time. Properly seasoned wood burns hotter, cleaner, and more efficiently. Our hardwoods are seasoned to perfection: dry, split to your specifications, and ready to burn the day they're delivered.
